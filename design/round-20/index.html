F150 cluster · Design round 20 · Wheel angle, grade and roll, heading, and altitude on Towing A

Towing A is locked as round 19 option A (ruler). Round 20 adds the wheel angle, the grade and roll (level page), the compass heading, and the altitude. The stock layouts (handoff/claude-dashboard ui1 to ui6) answer the firmware questions; the stock compass is a reference for the data only, not for the behaviour.

Pick: open. Live demo of the heading tape: tow-a.html?turn=1 (a steady turn through north).

  • Wheel angle: at crawl speed (8 mph and under) or in reverse, a steering bar takes the place of the speed scale. The fill runs from the center line to the wheel angle; the knob is a tire seen from above. No number. Field: angle, getAngle() (MCU message 0x26). Stock shows the angle in every gear while the factory setting "Wheel angle display" is on, so the MCU sends 0x26 in every gear. Positive = right.
  • Level page: a fifth page, at every speed, as on the stock layouts. Grade in % and roll in degrees. The grade moves here from the engine page, so one reading has one place; the engine page keeps rpm and boost (the left corner band also shows both). Climb, descent, and tilt jump the panel to the level page. Fields: mPitch and mRoll from the motion sensor in the cluster; the firmware sets the sign and the zero.
  • Tilt focus: at crawl speed or in reverse, a roll of 10° or more shows amber; 20° or more shows red. The motion sensor reads a fast corner as roll, so the tilt focus does not trigger above crawl speed. The thresholds are design values, to tune on the truck.
  • Heading tape: beside the clock, in the quiet grey, with N, E, S, and W brighter. At 5 mph and under the tape dims and holds the last heading.
  • Altitude: beside the outside temperature, in feet. Field: getAltitude(): the GPS altitude with a GPS fix, otherwise the barometric altitude.
  • A · Numbers: the grade and the roll as numbers with symbols, as on every other page. B · Truck images: the stock Ford side and rear images (clipped to the truck only), turned to the true angle over a dashed level line, each with one number.

Heading tape: why not the stock compass

  • Stock moves one tape image by getGpsBearing(), which turns bearings over 315° into negative values. A turn through NW sweeps the tape back across all 360°.
  • Each GPS fix (about one per second) starts a 600 ms move, then the tape stops. The tape steps instead of turning.
  • The bearing freezes at 5 mph and under without any sign. getCompassDir() also ends NW at 332.5° instead of 337.5°.

Firmware requirements for this tape:

  • Draw every mark by the angle difference modulo 360, so the tape has no ends and no seam.
  • Move the shown heading toward each new bearing the shorter way round: delta = (new − shown + 540) mod 360 − 180.
  • Ease toward the bearing every frame (time constant about 0.8 s), so the tape glides between fixes.
  • At 5 mph and under, hold the last heading and dim the tape.

Check: the demo turned through north for 9 s; the N mark moved at most 6.9 px per 100 ms, with no jump.
